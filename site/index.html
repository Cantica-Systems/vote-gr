Grand Rapids Precinct Lookup

Why does the State of Michigan make you hand over your personal info just to find out where to vote? That seemed unnecessary, so I built an alternative.

Type your address to find your ward, your precinct, and where to vote. The lookup is done client side in your browser, without querying any server to retrieve the information.

    Common questions

    What does this do?

    You type a Grand Rapids address and it gives you your ward, your precinct, and where that precinct votes, plus the early voting sites while they are open.

    The lookup is done client side, so no third-party database or address search is involved and no server is queried to answer it. The one exception is your own click: choosing Directions opens OpenStreetMap with your address as the route's starting point. Nothing is sent unless you click.

    Every answer is an estimate. I am fairly confident it is accurate in most cases, but your voter file is the source of truth, not a line on a map. Worst case you go to the wrong precinct by mistake, so plan ahead and double check.

    Why do this?

    Precinct lines and polling places changed in 2025, and the state's site is confusing and requests unnecessary information. Only looking up your own voter record, not searching a street address, returns your ward and precinct, which means handing over your name, birth month and year, and registration ZIP under a privacy notice that does not exempt the lookup from FOIA. So a question about a place becomes a record about a person, reachable by FOIA and exposed as well if the state's site were breached. This page asks for nothing.

    How does this work?

    Using parcel addresses, your browser loads a data file of every address in Grand Rapids already mapped to its ward and precinct, based on the State of Michigan's published precinct boundaries. Typing searches that file on your own device. The page tells you when it is unsure: when an address sits within a few meters of a precinct line, or when that exact house number is not in the file and the answer came from the houses either side of it.

    What are your data sources?

    Precinct boundaries come from the State of Michigan. Polling places come from the City Clerk's precinct directory. Early voting sites and hours come from the state's voter information center and the City Clerk. All public record, passed along as published. The scripts that regenerate these data files ship in the source code.
